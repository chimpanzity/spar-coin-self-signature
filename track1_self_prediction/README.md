# Track 1: static self-prediction and self-modelling

**Question:** can a language model recognise or predict its own behaviour better than it can recognise or predict another model's? And when it seems to, is that knowledge of *itself*, or only general knowledge about language models?

This track is the focus of the SPAR mid-term report. It moved through four phases:

## What track 1 shows so far

1. **Signatures exist; self-recognition does not.** Models produce distinctive behaviour (coin-flip statistics, favourite answers), and simple classifiers identify the source. But across attribution, forecasting and hypothetical self-prediction, no model reliably beats its own generic expectations about "a language model" when the question is about itself.
2. **Apparent self-knowledge is usually knowledge of procedures or defaults.** Fable's two strongest "self" results, independent-calls attribution (08) and octopus letters (18), are explained respectively by its belief about the stated generation procedure (10) and by a default answer it shares with "a typical model" (20).
3. **Models can reproduce their own outputs but not predict their properties.** They replay a sequence they would generate (16), yet single-position or single-letter questions draw on generic defaults (14–20).
4. **At the level of output probabilities, "predict yourself" changes the opening, not the generator** (21). In Qwen3-32B the self-prediction framing shifts the first-roll distribution away from what it actually generates. Given the same history, though, it continues exactly as direct generation does. The opening shift is **not** specific to *self*: asking about "another language model" in the same wrapper shifts it equally. The self wording adds only a smaller, opening-only difference.

## Open questions for the mid-term report

- **Finetuning:** would Binder-style finetuning on self vs cross predictions, which these untrained prompts did not attempt, produce a self-specific advantage?
- **Framing:** is the absence of self-specific prediction a property of the models, or of the question framing? For example, "you" vs a named model, or with or without visible reasoning.
- **The Qwen-high signal:** does the small self-specific effect in Qwen with high reasoning replicate, and does it depend on reasoning length?
