## Question

Experiment 21 showed Qwen3-32B cannot *predict* its own dice sequence. This pilot asks the Loula-style question instead: **can a model *recognize* its own sequences when shown them?**
- **Generation:** both models get the same instruction (Experiment 21's G prompt) at temperature 0.7, producing 50 valid sequences each.
- **Gate:** an external classifier must first show the two sources are distinguishable.
- **Recognition:** each model then picks "which sequence was generated by an instance of your model" from held-out Qwen/Mistral pairs. Named-model questions ("which was generated by Qwen3-32B / Mistral Small 3.2 24B") serve as generic source-identification controls.

## Results

- **Both models write "too random" dice sequences.**
  - Every sequence uses all six faces (a fair die: about 27%).
  - Mistral never repeats a face consecutively, and Qwen almost never does (a fair die: about 19%).
  - Both put a 4 at roll 6 in most sequences (70% / 64%).
  - Only 64 of the 100 strings are distinct, and one string was produced 4 times by each model.
- **The sources are distinguishable, modestly.** Development CV AUC is 0.76 (95% CI 0.54–0.92; permutation p = .002). On the held-out stimuli the frozen classifier reaches AUC 0.72 and gets 77.5% of pairs right.
- **The judges never use the sequences.**
  - Qwen answers "A" for self and named-Qwen and "B" for named-Mistral, every time. That follows the order the models are named in the prompt.
  - Mistral answers "A" in 115 of 120 trials.
  - Every condition therefore scores 0.50, apart from Mistral on named-Mistral at 0.525. No judge picks the same sequence after the A/B swap (0 of 20 pairs, except 3 of 20 for Mistral on named-Mistral).
  - The prespecified own-model advantage is +0.01 (95% −0.03 to +0.05).
- **Outcome D** (classifier succeeds, judges fail), in its most degenerate form. The elicitation measured response defaults, not recognition. This is neither evidence of absent self-recognition capacity nor of absent fingerprints.

Figures are in [`analysis/figures/`](analysis/figures/).

## Open questions and what it led to

**Recommendation: stop; do not scale this design.** Proposed, not run; each needs a separately approved protocol:
1. A same-format reading check ("which sequence has more 6s?"), as in [folder 09](../09_2026-10-01_ab_task_validation/).
2. Counterbalanced name order in the named prompt.
3. Scoring P(A) vs P(B) on open weights for both orientations (the Experiment 21 pipeline), which cancels position bias.

This connects to the earlier coin-flip result in [folder 02](../02_2026-09-29_pilot1_source_signature/): signatures exist, and there is no own-source advantage.
